# Algorithms and probability measures
## Annealing
The target at temperature beta is `p(tree, parameters) * likelihood^beta`. Prior draws start at beta=0. Curupixa's conditional-ESS calculation chooses the largest next temperature meeting the requested fraction; normalised particle weights determine whether resampling occurs. The sum of incremental log normalisers estimates log evidence. The schedule is adaptive, so no finite-particle unbiasedness guarantee is asserted here. Stopping before beta=1 returns a convergence error, rather than presenting an intermediate population as a posterior.
Each stage applies the specified number of MH proposals per particle. Prior independence proposals have acceptance ratio `likelihood_ratio^beta`, since the prior cancels. Local proposals choose a non-root internal vertex and one of its two NNI alternatives uniformly. Branch-length and relaxed-rate multipliers draw a symmetric change in log coordinates; the reverse/forward density ratio contributes the multiplier's log. Clock age moves are symmetric additive walks on internal node ages; proposals outside temporal or prior support are rejected. Tip ages remain zero in this initial phyloSMC implementation. Strict-clock rate moves are replaced by age moves.
Topology changes preserve the node-associated rate and age coordinates, with their full prior density evaluated on the proposed tree. In particular, autocorrelated rate probabilities are recalculated when ages or ancestry change. Hyperparameters are fixed; there is no implicit normalisation of rates to an observed tree mean.
## Non-clock prior
The prior is uniform over labelled rooted non-plane binary topologies, with probability `1/(2n-3)!!`, and independent exponential densities on the `2n-2` incoming non-root branches. The root stem contributes nothing. Under reversible models the likelihood does not identify a root, but this prior still defines a rooted parameterisation; it should not be mistaken for an independently specified unrooted-edge prior. Tree probabilities, rather than child-order probabilities, are used throughout.
## Clock prior
A clock analysis samples a dated tree from Curupixa's coalescent or conditioned Yule/birth–death prior, then samples branch rates from the supplied clock model. Trees retain time lengths; arrays record node ages and absolute branch rates. Likelihood evaluation supplies substitution lengths `(parent_age-child_age)*branch_rate` through Curupixa's existing edge override interface. It does not alter time lengths. Evidence includes the normalised dated-tree and rate priors with their supplied conditioning.
The independent lognormal model takes an arithmetic mean and a log standard deviation. The autocorrelated model is a conditional lognormal distribution between branch midpoints, with a fixed ancestral mean rate at the root. It uses constant branch rates for sequence evolution. These definitions are implemented in Curupixa's `molecular_clock.h`; they do not approximate a continuously integrated rate trajectory without saying so.
The mean clock rate and tree-prior time scale are fixed inputs. Without temporal information or informative scale assumptions, sequence data identify their product rather than separate absolute times and rates. The current program supports contemporary-tip tree priors, while Curupixa's clock calculations can also accept dated tips. Adding serial-tip priors and compatible online proposals requires separate work.
## Online addition
Previous particles must target the posterior for the old observations under the same fixed model and prior. The extended observations keep the old taxa in their original order and add one new name last. Sites, ambiguity weights and state alphabet must remain unchanged; compact pattern order may change. Unit site weights are required, and the implementation checks these conditions before drawing a proposal.
Choose uniformly among `2n-1` positions: every old incoming non-root edge, plus above the root. On a non-root edge of length L, choose a split fraction uniformly, retain total length L, and sample the new pendant length from the exponential prior. The proposal density for the pair of replacement lengths contains `1/L`. With exponential branch priors the prior/proposal ratio simplifies to `lambda*L`; omitting that term would bias the update. Above the root, sample both the edge to the old root and the new pendant edge independently from the prior, giving prior/proposal ratio one. The implementation evaluates the general prior difference and proposal density explicitly.
The new log weight is `old_log_weight + new_log_prior - old_log_prior - log_proposal + new_log_likelihood - old_log_likelihood`. Normalising it estimates the predictive evidence increment. The new population can be resampled and then undergoes MH moves at beta=1. Those moves can rearrange the old taxa too. This is one importance update, without an intermediate annealing bridge; a difficult arrival can therefore collapse weights. Guided placement, bridges and diversity diagnostics would improve that behaviour.
## Source organisation and extension
`src/phylosmc.c` implements stage sequencing and online importance updates. `src/particle.c` owns state construction, cloning and calls to Curupixa's prior/likelihood functions. `src/moves.c` implements resampling and MH proposals. `src/observations.c` handles explicit observation subsets and online consistency. The public header exposes small result structures; internal declarations sit in `src/smc_internal.h`.
Curupixa contains clock distributions and length conversion, tree insertion, log-weight normalisation, conditional ESS and resampling indices. It contains no particle population or SMC loop. New algorithms should reuse those calculations; proposal policy belongs here. The initial full-copy/full-likelihood implementation supplies a numerical reference for future cache updates and rollback. No acceleration is claimed yet.
